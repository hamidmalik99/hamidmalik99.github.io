Fractional CFO Website: What to Include to Win UK Clients

Hamid Ali AsadOctober 2026 · 7 min read

The short answer

A fractional CFO website should say clearly who you work with, which finance problems you solve, how engagements work and why you are credible. It needs service pages, a usable proof section, a booking link or short qualifying form, and for UK limited companies the required company details and a clear privacy notice.

Key takeaways

  • Your homepage should answer who you help, what you solve, how it works and why you, within the first minute.
  • Explaining your engagement model, even without exact fees, removes a common reason prospects delay.
  • Case studies should be permissioned or anonymised, and if you publish reviews the CMA expects a policy prohibiting fake reviews.
  • UK limited companies must show their registered number, registered office and place of registration on their website.
  • Forms that collect personal data need clear, easily accessible privacy information at the point of collection.

A fractional CFO website has one job: to make a founder or managing director confident enough to book a call. Most of your work probably comes through referrals and LinkedIn. But many people who hear your name will look you up before they get in touch. If your site is a single page with a stock photo of a calculator, you lose people who were already halfway to saying yes. This guide covers what a fractional CFO website should include, the UK legal basics it must get right, and how to turn visits into discovery calls. It's written for UK firms, but most of it applies anywhere.

What a prospect wants to know in the first minute

A business owner looking at fractional CFO support usually has a specific worry. Cash is tight, they're preparing to raise money, the bank wants better forecasts, or the bookkeeping has outgrown the founder. Your homepage should answer four questions quickly:

  1. Who do you work with? Name the stage, size or sector, for example "founder-led businesses with £2m to £20m turnover", not "businesses of all sizes".
  2. What problems do you solve? Cash flow forecasting, fundraising readiness, board reporting, pricing, systems, exit planning.
  3. How does it work? Days per month, retainer or project, what's included, how long a typical engagement runs.
  4. Why you? Your background, qualifications and the kind of businesses you've helped, described accurately.

If those four answers are clear above the fold, everything else on the site is supporting detail.

Pages every fractional CFO website should include

Services, written as outcomes

Give each main service its own page or section: cash flow forecasting, fundraising support, management accounts and board packs, finance function set-up, M&A or exit preparation. For each one, explain the problem, what you deliver and what the client needs to provide. "A rolling 13-week cash flow forecast, updated every fortnight, with a short call to walk through it" is more useful than "strategic cash management".

Who you work with

A short "ideal client" section helps the right people self-select and politely turns away the wrong ones. Sector focus, turnover range, funding stage and the kind of founder you work well with all belong here.

How engagements work and what they cost

Many firms hide pricing completely. You don't have to publish exact fees, but explaining the model helps a lot. Do you work on a monthly retainer, a set number of days, or fixed-price projects? Is there a minimum term? A simple "typical engagements" table removes a big reason people delay getting in touch.

About you, with real credentials

Owners are trusting you with their numbers. Show your professional qualifications, previous roles and sectors, and a real photo. If you hold a professional body membership, check that body's rules on how you describe yourself and your firm before you publish. Some professional titles have specific rules about how firms can use them.

Proof, handled carefully

Case studies are persuasive, but finance clients are often private about their numbers. Ask permission before naming a client, and consider anonymised case studies ("a SaaS business preparing for a Series A") if they'd rather not be named. Never invent results or testimonials.

If you show reviews or star ratings on your website, be aware of the UK rules. The Competition and Markets Authority's short guide for businesses publishing consumer reviews says traders who publish reviews should have a published policy that prohibits fake reviews and sets out their approach to incentivised reviews. They must also take reasonable and proportionate steps to prevent and remove banned content. The full CMA fake reviews guidance explains this in more detail. Check how it applies to your firm.

A contact page that books calls

The best fractional CFO websites make the next step obvious: book a 20–30 minute discovery call. Use a booking link or a short form, not just an email address. If you use a form, ask a few qualifying questions: turnover band, what prompted them to look for help, and their timeline. UK legal basics your website must get right

These details are easy to overlook, and they aren't optional.

Company details on your website

If you trade through a limited company, GOV.UK's guidance on signs, stationery and promotional material says your website must show:

  • the company's registered number
  • its registered office address
  • where the company is registered (England and Wales, Scotland or Northern Ireland)
  • the fact that it's a limited company, usually by giving the full name including "Limited" or "Ltd"

It also says that if you choose to include directors' names, you must list all of them. Most firms put these details in the website footer.

A proper privacy notice

Your contact form and booking tool collect personal data, so you need privacy information. The ICO's guidance on the right to be informed says you must give people privacy information at the time you collect their data. This includes your purposes, your lawful basis, how long you keep the data and who you share it with. It must be concise, transparent and in clear, plain language. The ICO says you can meet this by putting the information on your website, but you must make people aware of it and give them an easy way to reach it. A link next to the form's submit button is a simple way to do that.

The ICO also notes that its guidance is under review following the Data (Use and Access) Act, so check the current version. None of this is legal advice.

Getting found: SEO for fractional CFOs

Referrals will probably remain your main source of work. But a well-structured site also helps you show up when someone searches "fractional CFO" plus their city or sector, or asks an AI assistant who to talk to.

  • Write sector pages if you specialise, such as "fractional CFO for SaaS businesses" or "fractional finance director for manufacturers". Fill them with genuine detail about that sector's finance problems.
  • Answer real questions in articles: when a business needs a fractional CFO, how it differs from an accountant, and what a first 90 days looks like.
  • Use clear, quotable answers. Short, direct answers near the top of a page help both Google and AI tools. My guide to GEO and AEO explains how.
  • Keep your Google Business Profile accurate if you serve a local area. Use your real business name and the services you actually offer.

Frequently asked questions

What should a fractional CFO put on their website?

Explain who you work with, the finance problems you solve, how engagements work and your credentials. Add a page or section for each service, permissioned or anonymised case studies, and a booking link or short form for discovery calls. UK limited companies also need their company details on the site and a clear privacy notice.

Should fractional CFOs publish their prices?

You don't have to publish exact fees, but explaining your model helps. Say whether you work on a monthly retainer, a set number of days or fixed-price projects, whether there is a minimum term, and what a typical engagement includes. That gives prospects enough to decide whether to book a call.

What company information must a UK website show?

GOV.UK says a limited company's website must show its registered number, registered office address, where it is registered (England and Wales, Scotland or Northern Ireland) and that it is a limited company. If you choose to include directors' names, you must list all of them. Most firms put these details in the footer.

How do fractional CFOs get clients online?

Most still rely on referrals and networks, but a clear website supports them by converting people who look you up. Sector pages, articles answering real questions, a qualifying contact form and an accurate Google Business Profile can also help you appear when owners search for fractional CFO support.
