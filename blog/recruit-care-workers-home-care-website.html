How to Recruit Care Workers Through Your Home Care Website

Hamid Ali AsadOctober 2026 · 6 min read

The short answer

To recruit care workers through your website, build a careers page that states pay, mileage and travel time, explains how rotas work, lists areas and driving needs, and describes training and support. Explain pre-employment checks clearly, give each vacancy its own page with job posting markup, and keep the first application step short.

Key takeaways

  • A careers page lets you answer the questions care workers use to compare agencies, such as pay, travel and rotas.
  • The CQC expects competence to be assessed before staff work unsupervised, so explaining induction and shadowing reassures new starters.
  • Employers must check right to work before employing someone, and must not discriminate based on where people are from.
  • Give each vacancy its own page with job posting structured data, and remove closed jobs promptly.
  • A short, phone-friendly first application step with a clear next step reduces drop-offs.

If you run a domiciliary care agency, you probably spend as much time trying to recruit care workers as you do winning new clients. Job boards bring applications, but they present every agency the same way: an hourly rate, a postcode and a list of duties. Your own website is where you can show candidates why your agency is a better place to work, and where you can make applying quick and simple. This guide covers what a care agency careers page should include, how to get your vacancies into Google, and how the UK's recruitment rules for care providers should shape the way you describe the process.

Why your website matters when you recruit care workers

Care workers compare agencies. They look at pay, mileage, how rotas work, how much travel is involved, what training they'll get and whether the agency treats staff well. Job adverts have limited space for all that. A good careers page answers those questions in full, and a candidate who has read it before applying already knows what to expect.

Your website can also bring in candidates directly. Google's job posting documentation explains that adding job posting structured data to your vacancy pages makes them eligible to appear in Google's job search experience, where people can filter by location and job title. A local care worker searching for "care assistant jobs" plus your town can find your vacancy without going through a job board.

What a care agency careers page should include

Pay, mileage and travel, stated clearly

Be specific: hourly rate or range, how you pay for travel time and mileage, whether weekend and bank holiday rates differ, and how often you pay. Vague phrases like "competitive rates" don't help a candidate compare you with the agency down the road.

How rotas work

Explain how rotas are planned, how far in advance staff know their shifts, whether they can choose set areas or set clients, and how you handle short-notice changes. A predictable rota close to home can matter to candidates as much as pay.

Driving and areas covered

Say whether a full UK driving licence and access to a car are needed, and list the towns and areas your care workers cover. If you have walking rounds in some areas, say so; it widens your pool of applicants.

Training and progression

Describe your induction, shadowing before working alone, ongoing training and any support towards qualifications. The CQC's guidance on Regulation 19 says providers should assess the competence of employees before they work unsupervised, and provide supervision until they're assessed as competent. It also says providers that employ social care support workers are expected to follow the Care Certificate standards to assess competence. Explaining this on your careers page reassures new starters, especially those new to care, that they won't be sent out alone before they're ready.

What the work is really like

Describe a typical day honestly: the kinds of visits, the support people need, and how you support staff when visits are difficult. Short quotes from your own care workers, used with permission, are more convincing than stock photos.

Who you're looking for

If you welcome people without previous care experience and train them, say so clearly. If some roles need experience or particular skills, explain which.

Be clear about checks, without putting people off

Candidates will need to go through pre-employment checks, and it's better to explain them upfront than to surprise people later.

Right to work

GOV.UK's guidance on checking a job applicant's right to work says employers must check that an applicant is allowed to work for them in the UK before employing them. Checks can be done online using the applicant's share code, by checking original documents, or by using an identity service provider. The same page reminds employers that they must not discriminate against anyone because of where they're from.

On your careers page, a simple line works: "Before you start, we'll check your right to work in the UK. We'll tell you what documents to bring." If you can't offer visa sponsorship, say so clearly and neutrally.

Fit and proper checks

The CQC's Regulation 19 page explains that providers must operate robust recruitment procedures, including relevant checks, to make sure the people they employ are fit and proper, with the qualifications, competence, skills and experience needed for the role. It notes that "person employed" includes volunteers, contractors, and agency or bank staff.

The same GOV.UK right to work page, as part of its employer step-by-step guide, points employers to find out which Disclosure and Barring Service (DBS) check is right for an employee, for example if they'll be working in healthcare. Tell candidates which checks you'll carry out, roughly how long they take and whether you pay for them.

This isn't legal advice. Check the current guidance from GOV.UK and the CQC when you write your recruitment process.

Getting your vacancies into Google's job search

Google's job posting documentation lists the required properties for job posting structured data as the job title, description, date posted, hiring organisation and job location. It recommends adding salary, employment type and a closing date. A few rules matter:

  • One vacancy per page. Google says job posting structured data must only appear on a page dedicated to a single job, not a list of vacancies.
  • Remove closed jobs promptly. Expired jobs must be removed or marked as expired, and Google warns that not doing so may lead to a manual action.
  • Salary must be the actual salary. Google notes that only employers can provide it, and it shouldn't be an estimate.

On WordPress, a simple "Vacancies" section with a form for your office team to add each role can generate this markup automatically.

Make applying quick

Candidates often apply from a phone, between visits or after a shift. A long application form loses them.

  • Short first step: name, phone number, postcode, whether they drive, and when they can start.
  • Optional CV upload rather than a required one.
  • A clear next step: "We'll call you within two working days to arrange a chat."
  • A privacy note next to the form explaining how you'll use their details, with a link to your privacy notice. The ICO's guidance on the right to be informed says people must get privacy information at the time you collect their data.

Collect the full application, references and employment history at the next stage, when the candidate is engaged.

Frequently asked questions

What should a care agency careers page include?

Clear pay, mileage and travel-time details, how rotas are planned, the areas covered and whether driving is needed, induction and training including shadowing, an honest description of the work, quotes from your own staff with permission, an explanation of pre-employment checks, a page for each vacancy and a short application form.

How can a home care agency get its jobs on Google?

Give each vacancy its own page and add Google's job posting structured data, with the title, description, date posted, hiring organisation and location, plus salary and closing date where possible. Don't put the markup on a page listing several jobs, and remove or expire vacancies promptly once they close.

What checks do care workers need before starting?

Employers must check a person's right to work in the UK before employing them, online with a share code, by checking original documents or through an identity service provider. CQC Regulation 19 requires robust recruitment procedures and relevant checks so staff are fit and proper. GOV.UK also points employers to the right DBS check for healthcare roles.

How do I make applying easy for care workers?

Keep the first step short enough to complete on a phone: name, phone number, postcode, whether they drive and when they can start, with an optional CV upload. Tell them when you'll call, add a short privacy note with a link to your privacy notice, and collect full details at the next stage.
