Local SEO for Lifting Equipment Hire and Inspection Branches

Hamid Ali AsadNovember 2026 · 7 min read

The short answer

Local SEO for lifting equipment hire and inspection businesses means a Google Business Profile for each staffed depot or team location, using the same business name without keywords, service areas for mobile inspection teams, a branch page for each location with matching details and structured data, a clear LOLER inspection page and reviews.

Key takeaways

  • Google ranks local results on relevance, distance and prominence, so each genuine branch needs its own presence.
  • Create one profile per staffed depot or team location; virtual offices aren't eligible.
  • Use the same business name on every profile, with no service or location keywords.
  • Link each profile to its own branch page with matching details and LocalBusiness structured data.
  • Explain inspection services clearly, summarising HSE guidance on thorough examination intervals and linking to it.

When a site manager needs a chain block hired by tomorrow, or a contractor's LOLER examinations are due next week, they rarely search for a company by name. They search "lifting equipment hire near me" or "LOLER inspection [town]", and the depots that appear in Google's map results get the call. Local SEO for lifting equipment hire and inspection businesses is about making sure each of your branches is one of them. This guide covers how to set up Google Business Profiles for depots and mobile inspection teams, how to build branch pages that back them up, and how to explain your inspection services clearly. It's written for UK suppliers.

How Google chooses local results

Google's guide to improving your local ranking says local results are mainly based on relevance, distance and prominence. Relevance is how well your profile matches the search; distance is how far you are from the searcher or the place they searched for; prominence is how well known you are, including reviews and links to your website. The same page says there's no way to pay for a better local ranking.

For a multi-branch supplier, distance is the key point: a single head office profile can't be close to customers in every town you serve. Each genuine branch needs its own presence.

Google Business Profiles for depots and inspection teams

Google's guidelines for representing your business set the rules. The points that matter most for lifting equipment businesses:

Depots customers visit

If customers collect hire equipment or buy over the counter at a depot, that depot can have its own profile at its real address. Google says businesses showing their address should have permanent fixed signage with their business name at that address, and that you shouldn't create more than one profile for each location.

Mobile inspection and service teams

If your engineers travel to customers' sites for thorough examinations or repairs, that's a service-area business. Google says that if you have different locations with separate service areas and separate staff at each, you can have one profile for each location, and that the overall service area shouldn't extend further than about two hours' driving time from where the business is based.

A depot that also sends engineers out can be what Google calls a hybrid business: show the depot address and set a service area, as long as the depot is staffed and can receive customers during its stated hours.

What isn't allowed

Google says a virtual office isn't eligible for a profile, and a co-working desk only counts if it has signage, receives customers and is staffed during opening hours. Don't create profiles for towns where you have no staffed location.

Names and categories

Google's guidelines say all locations of a business in the same country should use the same name, such as "Example Lifting" rather than "Example Lifting Leeds Depot", unless the real-world name consistently varies. Adding service or location keywords to a business name isn't permitted and can lead to suspension.

Choose the fewest categories that describe what each location is, using Google's test of categories that complete the sentence "this business IS a…". Google also says all locations that provide the same service should share the category that best represents the business.

Phone numbers and hours

Google asks for a phone number that connects to the individual location, and a local number rather than a central call centre where possible. Set each depot's real customer-facing opening hours.

Branch pages on your website

Each Business Profile should link to a page on your website for that branch, not just your homepage. A good branch page gives Google and customers the detail a profile can't.

What to include on each branch page

  • The branch name, full address, phone number and opening hours, matching the Business Profile exactly
  • A map and directions, including vehicle access and parking for collections
  • What's available at that depot: hire stock, sales counter, test bay, repair workshop
  • The areas the mobile team covers from that branch
  • The local team, with their permission
  • Local work, such as types of sites you support, described without naming clients unless they've agreed
  • A clear next step: request a hire quote, book an inspection, or call the branch

Avoid copying the same text across every branch page with only the town name changed. My guide to service area pages covers Google's spam policies on near-duplicate location pages, and the same rules apply here.

Structured data for each branch

Google's documentation on local business structured data explains that it lets you tell Google about business hours, different departments within a business and more. Its required properties are the business name and address. Add LocalBusiness markup to each branch page with that branch's details, and test it with Google's Rich Results Test.

Explaining your inspection services

Customers searching for inspections often know they need "LOLER" but not much more. A clear inspection services page, linked from every branch page, answers their questions and shows you know the rules.

The HSE's page on thorough examinations and inspections of lifting equipment explains that, unless there's an examination scheme specifying other intervals, thorough examinations should be carried out every:

  • 6 months for lifting equipment and accessories used to lift people
  • 6 months for all lifting accessories
  • 12 months for all other lifting equipment

It also explains that examinations are needed before first use in some cases, after assembly at each new location for equipment such as tower cranes, and after exceptional circumstances such as damage, long periods out of use or major changes. It says thorough examinations must be carried out by a competent person, who completes a written report containing the information required by LOLER Schedule 1.

On your page, summarise the rules accurately, link to the HSE, and explain what your service includes: what you examine, how reports are delivered, how you remind customers when examinations are due, and how quickly each branch can attend. Tell customers to check the HSE guidance for their own equipment. Don't overstate what the law requires.

Reviews from trade customers

Google's local ranking page says more reviews and positive ratings can help a business's local ranking. Ask regular hire and inspection customers to review the branch they deal with, using a review link for that specific profile. Google's tips on getting more reviews say offering incentives for reviews is prohibited, so don't offer discounts in exchange. Reply to reviews professionally and keep customer details out of replies.

Common mistakes

  • Keywords in the business name. Google doesn't permit service or location keywords in profile names and may suspend profiles that use them.
  • Profiles for towns without a staffed branch. Virtual offices aren't eligible.
  • All profiles linking to the homepage. Link each one to its branch page.
  • Inconsistent details. Name, address, phone and hours should match on the profile, the branch page and the structured data.
  • Vague inspection pages. "We do LOLER testing" tells customers little. Explain what you examine, how reports arrive and how you handle defects.

Frequently asked questions

Should each lifting equipment depot have its own Google Business Profile?

Google allows one profile per real location. A depot with signage that's staffed and receives customers can have its own profile. Mobile teams based at separate locations with their own staff and service areas can also have one each. Don't create profiles for towns where you have no staffed location, because virtual offices aren't eligible.

Can I add keywords like LOLER testing to my business name on Google?

No. Google's guidelines say your profile name should match your real-world business name, and adding service or location information isn't permitted and can lead to suspension. Use the same name on every branch and describe services in your categories, description and website instead.

How often do lifting equipment thorough examinations need to happen?

The HSE says that, unless an examination scheme sets other intervals, thorough examinations should be every 6 months for equipment used to lift people and for all lifting accessories, and every 12 months for other lifting equipment. Examinations may also be needed after assembly, damage or long periods out of use. Check the HSE guidance.

What should a lifting equipment branch page include?

Include the branch name, address, phone and hours matching its Google profile, a map and access details, the stock and services at that depot, the areas its mobile team covers, the local team with permission, and a clear way to request a hire quote or book an inspection. Add LocalBusiness structured data too.
