Structural Engineer Enquiry Form: What to Ask Before You Quote

Hamid Ali AsadOctober 2026 · 7 min read

The short answer

A structural engineer enquiry form should ask for contact details, the client's role, site location, job type, project stage, drawings or photos, and timing, with optional notes. Leave out data you don't need to quote, add a short privacy note with a link to your policy, and tell people when you'll reply.

Key takeaways

  • A structured fee proposal form gets you the details to price a job without rounds of emails.
  • Ask for role, site location, job type, project stage, files and timing; keep free text optional.
  • The ICO's data minimisation principle says collect only what you need, not data that might be useful later.
  • UK and Australian privacy guidance both point to telling people at the point of collection why you need their details.
  • Use the form's answers to route enquiries automatically and set clear expectations about your reply.

Most structural engineering websites end with the same form: name, email, phone, message. Then the back-and-forth starts. Where is the property? What kind of job is it? Are there drawings? Has the builder been appointed? By the time you have enough to price the work, the client may have accepted a quote from someone who asked the right questions first. A well-designed structural engineer enquiry form fixes that. It collects what you need to prepare a fee proposal in one go, filters out jobs you don't do, and shows the client you know what you're doing. This guide covers what to ask, what not to ask, and the privacy basics for firms in Australia and the UK.

Why the standard contact form costs you work

A blank "message" box puts the work on the client, and many clients don't know what an engineer needs. You get "Need an engineer for an extension, please call", and the job starts with a phone call or three emails before you can even estimate the fee.

A structured fee proposal request form changes that. The client answers a few specific questions, attaches what they have, and you can reply with a fee or a short list of follow-up questions. It also helps you sort enquiries: a homeowner wanting a wall removed and a developer with a multi-storey project need very different responses.

What to ask on a structural engineer enquiry form

Keep it short enough to finish on a phone, and ask only what changes your fee or your decision to quote.

1. Who the client is

  • Name, email and phone
  • Their role: homeowner, builder, architect or designer, developer, strata or body corporate, other

The role tells you how they'll brief you and who else is involved. An architect will usually send drawings; a homeowner may need guidance on what to provide.

2. Where the site is

  • Site address or suburb/town and postcode

Location affects travel time, local council or building control requirements, and whether the job is in your service area at all. If you only cover certain areas, say so next to this field.

3. What the job is

A dropdown or checkboxes work better than free text here. Typical options:

  • Wall removal or opening
  • Extension or alteration
  • New build
  • Inspection or structural report (for example cracking, movement, pre-purchase)
  • Retaining wall
  • Remedial or repair design
  • Certification or sign-off
  • Civil: drainage, subdivision, driveways or similar
  • Other (with a short description)

4. What stage the project is at

  • Just exploring
  • Architectural drawings in progress
  • Drawings complete, ready for engineering
  • Builder appointed or works started
  • Works complete, need inspection or certification

Stage tells you urgency and scope. A job where works have started and the builder is waiting is very different from early design.

5. Files

  • Upload drawings, plans or photos (PDF, JPG, PNG, DWG if you accept it)

For inspections, photos of cracks or defects are often very useful. Set a sensible file size limit and say what it is.

6. Timing

  • When do you need the engineering by?

7. A short description

  • Anything else we should know?

Put this last and keep it optional. By now the structured answers have covered the essentials.

What not to ask

Extra fields can put people off finishing the form, and collecting data you don't need creates privacy obligations.

The UK's data minimisation principle is a useful test even if you're in Australia. The ICO's guidance on data minimisation says personal data should be adequate, relevant and limited to what is necessary for your purpose. It adds that you must not collect personal data on the off-chance that it might be useful in the future.

For an enquiry form, that usually means leaving out:

  • date of birth
  • full postal address of the client when you only need the site address
  • budget, unless it genuinely affects whether you'll quote
  • marketing preferences bundled into a required field

Privacy notices: UK and Australia

UK

The ICO's guidance on the right to be informed says you must give people privacy information at the time you collect their data. That includes your purposes, lawful basis, retention period and who you share the data with, in clear and plain language. A short note under the form with a link to your full privacy notice is a practical way to do this.

Australia

The OAIC's guidelines on APP 5 explain that an APP entity collecting personal information must take reasonable steps to notify the person of certain matters, or ensure they're aware of them. These include its identity and contact details, the purposes of collection, its usual disclosures and information about its privacy policy. These steps should be taken at or before the time of collection where practicable.

For online forms, the OAIC's examples of reasonable steps include clearly and prominently displaying these matters in the form, or providing a readily accessible and prominent link to a notice. Whether the Privacy Act applies to your firm depends on your circumstances, so check the OAIC's current guidance. This isn't legal advice.

Either way, the practical step is the same: a brief, plain-English note under the form explaining why you're collecting the details and what you'll do with them, plus a link to your privacy policy.

Example: before and after

Here's an invented example of the same enquiry coming through two different forms.

Before (basic contact form): "Hi, need an engineer for my house. Can you call me? – Sam"

After (structured form):

Field Answer
Role Homeowner
Site Suburb and postcode within your service area
Job type Wall removal or opening
Stage Architectural drawings complete
Files Floor plan PDF, two photos of the wall
Timing Builder starting in six weeks
Notes Load-bearing wall between kitchen and dining room

With the second version you can check the drawings and estimate the fee straight away, often without needing a call first.

Making the form work harder

Route enquiries automatically

Use the job type and stage answers to send each enquiry to the right engineer, tag it in your CRM and trigger the right reply. An inspection request might get an automatic email asking for more photos, while a new build goes straight to a senior engineer. My guide to automating lead triage into your CRM shows how.

Set expectations after submitting

The confirmation message and email should say what happens next and roughly when you'll reply, for example "We'll review your drawings and send a fee proposal or questions within two working days". Then stick to it.

Put the form where people decide

Frequently asked questions

What information does a structural engineer need to quote?

Usually the site location, the type of job, the project stage, any architectural drawings or photos, the client's role and the timing. With those details an engineer can often estimate the fee or send a short list of follow-up questions, instead of starting with a phone call to gather the basics.

Should a structural engineering enquiry form allow file uploads?

Yes. Drawings and photos often tell an engineer more than a written description, especially for inspections of cracking or movement. Accept common formats such as PDF and JPG, set a sensible file size limit, and say what it is next to the upload field so clients aren't left guessing.

Do I need a privacy notice on my website contact form?

In the UK, the ICO says you must give people privacy information when you collect their data. In Australia, the OAIC's APP 5 guidelines describe notifying people at or before collection, for example by displaying the information in the form or linking prominently to a notice. A short note under the form with a link to your policy covers this.

How many fields should an engineering enquiry form have?

Only as many as change your fee or your decision to quote. For most structural and civil firms that's contact details, role, site location, job type, project stage, file upload and timing, plus an optional notes box. Leave out fields such as date of birth or budget unless you genuinely need them.
