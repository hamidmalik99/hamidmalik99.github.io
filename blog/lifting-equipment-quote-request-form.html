Lifting Equipment Quote Request Form: What to Ask Online

Hamid Ali AsadNovember 2026 · 7 min read

The short answer

A lifting equipment quote request form should use a product type selector with conditional fields, asking only for the details estimators need, such as working load limit, length and fittings, plus delivery date and documents needed. Allow photo and drawing uploads, add a privacy note, acknowledge every request and route it to the right estimator.

Key takeaways

  • Bespoke slings and beams need a dedicated quote form, not a generic contact form.
  • Conditional fields by product type keep the form short and relevant.
  • Let customers upload photos and drawings easily from a phone.
  • The CAP Code says 'from' prices must not mislead and ex-VAT prices need a clear VAT statement.
  • Collect only the personal data you need and give privacy information at the point of collection.

Not everything a lifting equipment supplier sells fits in a shopping basket. Made-to-length chain and wire rope slings, lifting beams, spreader beams and other bespoke items need a quote, and the quote needs details. When the website's only option is "Contact us", your sales team spends the first call collecting information that a good form could have gathered. A well-built lifting equipment quote request form asks the right questions up front, works for buyers on site with a phone, and gets the enquiry to the right person. This guide covers what to ask, how to keep the form short, what to say about prices, how to handle personal data, and how to connect quotes to orders. It's written for UK suppliers.

Why bespoke items need their own quote form

A generic contact form treats a request for a 20-tonne lifting beam the same as a question about opening hours. Your team then has to go back to the customer for the load, the dimensions, the lifting points and the delivery date before anyone can price it.

A dedicated quote form for bespoke work:

  • collects the details your estimators always need
  • lets customers attach drawings and photos
  • routes the enquiry to the right person or branch
  • gives you a record of every request, so nothing is lost in an inbox

What to ask on a lifting equipment quote request form

Ask your estimators which details they always need and which they chase most often. The fields below are a starting point; adapt them to your products.

About the customer

  • name, company, email and phone
  • whether they already have a trade account
  • delivery location or branch for collection

About the item

Use a product type selector, then show only the questions that apply:

Product type Typical questions
Chain or wire rope slings Number of legs, working load limit needed, length, top and bottom fittings, quantity
Webbing or roundslings Working load limit, effective length, quantity
Lifting or spreader beams Load to be lifted, span or lifting point spacing, headroom available, attachment method
Other bespoke items Free-text description and drawings

About the job

  • what's being lifted, and in what environment, if relevant
  • when the item is needed
  • any documents required with delivery, such as certificates

Attachments

Let customers upload drawings, photos of the load or existing equipment, and specifications. Many buyers are on site, so make uploading from a phone camera easy.

Keep the form short with conditional fields

Long forms put people off. Conditional fields keep it manageable: a customer choosing "chain sling" sees chain sling questions, not beam questions. Mark only the details you genuinely need as required, and give an "I'm not sure" option where a customer might not know the answer yet. Your estimator can fill the gaps on the call.

Documents and certification

Buyers of lifting equipment often need paperwork alongside the item, so ask which documents they need with delivery, and say what you supply as standard.

The HSE's guidance on thorough examinations and inspections of lifting equipment explains that lifting equipment needs a thorough examination before first use, unless it has a Declaration of Conformity less than one year old and wasn't assembled on site. It also says records should be kept of thorough examinations and inspections, and of the Declarations of Conformity for lifting equipment and accessories.

On your quote form and quotes, make clear what documents come with each item. Don't tell customers what the law requires of them; link to the HSE guidance and let them check. If you provide documents online, my guide to putting lifting equipment certificates online covers building a customer portal.

What to say about prices

If you show indicative prices for bespoke items, such as "slings from £X", the CAP Code's rules on misleading advertising apply. They say "from" and "up to" price claims must not mislead by exaggerating how available the price is, and that quoted prices must include non-optional fees and charges that apply to all or most buyers.

The same rules say VAT-exclusive prices may be given if everyone the price is clearly addressed to pays no VAT or can recover it, and that they must come with a prominent statement of the VAT payable. If trade customers see ex-VAT prices, label them clearly. My guide to WooCommerce trade accounts covers VAT display in more detail.

If you don't show prices, tell customers how quickly you'll respond, and only promise a turnaround you can keep.

Handling personal data on the form

A quote form collects names, emails and phone numbers, so UK GDPR applies. The ICO's guidance on data minimisation explains that you should collect only what you need for your purpose. Its guidance on the right to be informed says people should get privacy information at the time you collect their data, so add a short note and a link to your privacy notice under the form.

Uploaded drawings and photos can also contain information, so store them securely and delete them when you no longer need them.

From quote to order

The form is the start of the process, not the end:

  1. Send an automatic acknowledgement with a reference number and the expected response time.
  2. Route the enquiry by product type or branch to the right estimator.
  3. Log it in your CRM or quoting system, so nothing is lost and you can follow up.
  4. Send the quote with a clear way to accept it, such as a link to accept online or convert it into a trade account order.
  5. Follow up on quotes that go quiet, a few days after sending.

Before and after: a quote request

Here's an invented example.

Before: A contact form with "Name, Email, Message". The customer writes "Need a price for a 2-leg chain sling", and the estimator has to call back for the working load limit, length, fittings and quantity.

After: The customer chooses "Chain sling", enters 2 legs, the working load limit, length, top and bottom fittings and quantity, uploads a photo of the existing sling, and picks a delivery date. The enquiry goes straight to the branch estimator, who can price it without a call.

Common mistakes

  • One generic contact form for everything. Bespoke enquiries arrive with missing details.
  • Too many required fields. Customers on site give up. Use conditional fields and "not sure" options.
  • No file uploads. Drawings and photos often answer questions faster than text.
  • Unrealistic "from" prices or response times. Only show what most customers can actually get.
  • Quotes that live only in email. Log every request so you can follow up.

Frequently asked questions

What should a lifting equipment quote request form ask?

Start with contact details, account status and delivery location. Then use a product type selector to show relevant questions, such as legs, working load limit, length, fittings and quantity for slings, or load, span and headroom for beams. Add the date needed, documents required with delivery and file uploads.

How do I stop a quote form being too long?

Use conditional fields so customers only see questions for the product they choose, make only essential fields required, and offer an 'I'm not sure' option where a customer might not know the answer. Your estimator can fill the gaps on a call, which is better than losing the enquiry altogether.

Can I show 'from' prices for bespoke lifting equipment?

You can, but the CAP Code says 'from' prices must not mislead by exaggerating how available the price is, and quoted prices must include non-optional fees that apply to most buyers. If you show ex-VAT prices to trade customers, include a prominent statement of the VAT payable.

Do I need a privacy notice on a quote request form?

A quote form collects personal data such as names, emails and phone numbers, so UK GDPR applies. The ICO says people should get privacy information when you collect their data, so add a short note and a link to your privacy notice under the form, and collect only what you need.
