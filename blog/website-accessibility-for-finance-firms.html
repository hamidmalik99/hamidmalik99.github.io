Website Accessibility for Finance Firms: ADA, WCAG and What to Fix

Hamid Ali AsadNovember 2026 · 6 min read

The short answer

Website accessibility for finance firms means making your site usable with screen readers, keyboards and zoom. The DOJ says the ADA applies to the websites of businesses open to the public and points to WCAG as guidance. Fix contrast, color-only cues, missing alt text, unlabeled forms, mouse-only menus, missing captions and untagged PDFs, then test manually.

Key takeaways

  • The DOJ takes the position that the ADA applies to the online services of businesses open to the public.
  • There's no detailed DOJ technical standard for business websites, but WCAG is the guidance it points to.
  • WCAG 2.2 Level AA is a common target, and content meeting 2.2 also meets 2.1 and 2.0.
  • Forms, booking widgets and client portals are often the least accessible part of a finance firm's site.
  • The DOJ warns a clean automated report doesn't mean a site is accessible, so pair tools with manual checks.

Website accessibility for finance firms is easy to put off. Your clients are business owners, not the general public, and your site is mostly service pages and a contact form. But some of your prospects and clients use screen readers, keyboard navigation or zoom, and an inaccessible site quietly turns them away. In the US, the Department of Justice also takes the position that the ADA applies to the websites of businesses open to the public. This guide explains what the official guidance says, which accessibility problems are most common on fractional CFO, bookkeeping and accounting firm websites, and a practical plan to fix them.

What the US guidance says

The Department of Justice's guidance on web accessibility and the ADA explains that Title III of the ADA prohibits discrimination against people with disabilities by businesses open to the public. It states that the Department has consistently taken the position that the ADA's requirements apply to the goods, services and activities offered by public accommodations, including those offered on the web.

The guidance says the Department does not have a regulation setting out detailed technical standards for business websites, so businesses currently have flexibility in how they make online services accessible. It points to existing standards, including the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG), as helpful guidance. Among its sample cases is an agreement with HRB Digital and HRB Tax Group (H&R Block) over claims that its website wasn't coded to work with assistive technology such as screen readers and keyboard navigation.

The guidance notes it is informal and doesn't have the force of law, and whether and how the ADA applies to a particular firm depends on the facts. Take legal advice for your situation. This isn't legal advice.

WCAG in plain English

The W3C's WCAG overview explains that WCAG is the international standard for making web content accessible. WCAG 2.2 has 13 guidelines organized under four principles: perceivable, operable, understandable and robust. Each guideline has testable success criteria at three levels: A, AA and AAA.

WCAG 2.0, 2.1 and 2.2 all remain published standards, and they're designed to be backwards compatible: content that meets WCAG 2.2 also meets 2.1 and 2.0. The W3C encourages using the latest version, and Level AA is a common target for business websites.

Common problems on finance firm websites

The DOJ guidance lists examples of barriers, and several are common on professional services sites:

Poor color contrast

Light gray text on white, or white text over a photo, is hard to read for people with limited vision. Some brand palettes look elegant but fail on contrast. Check body text, buttons and form labels.

Color used alone to give information

Red text to mark required form fields, or a green or red figure in a chart with no label, means nothing to someone who can't distinguish those colors or uses a screen reader. The DOJ suggests adding text cues, such as the word "required".

Images and charts without text alternatives

Finance sites often use charts, infographics and photos of the team. Each meaningful image needs short, descriptive alt text. Charts that carry information need a text summary or a data table nearby.

Inaccessible forms

Discovery call forms, proposal requests and booking widgets are where finance firms win clients, and they're often the least accessible part of the site. The DOJ guidance highlights labels that screen readers can announce, clear instructions, and error messages that say what went wrong and how to fix it. Third-party booking tools and client portal logins need checking too.

Mouse-only navigation

Menus that only open on hover, pop-ups that can't be closed with the keyboard, and sliders without controls block keyboard users. Every link, menu and form field should be reachable and usable with the Tab key, with a visible focus outline.

Videos without captions

If you publish explainer videos or recorded webinars, add accurate captions.

Documents

PDF guides, engagement letters and downloadable checklists are part of your web content. The W3C notes WCAG can be applied to non-web documents as well. Tag PDFs properly, or offer an accessible HTML version.

A practical plan to improve accessibility

Step What to do Why
1. Set a target Aim for WCAG 2.2 Level AA The latest version and a common target level
2. Run automated checks Use an accessibility checker on key pages Finds many common issues quickly
3. Test manually Keyboard-only navigation, zoom to 200%, a screen reader on your forms A clean automated report doesn't guarantee accessibility
4. Fix templates first Header, menu, footer, buttons, form styles One fix covers every page
5. Fix content Alt text, headings, link text, captions, PDFs Page-by-page improvements
6. Check third-party tools Booking widgets, portals, chat tools Your clients use them as part of your site
7. Publish a statement Accessibility statement with a contact route DOJ guidance mentions a way to report problems
8. Review regularly Check new pages and plugins before launch Accessibility slips without upkeep

On automated tools, the DOJ guidance is direct: automated checkers and overlays can be helpful, but a "clean" report doesn't necessarily mean everything is accessible, and pairing a manual check with automated checkers gives a better picture. Don't rely on an overlay widget alone.

Common accessibility mistakes to avoid

  • Treating accessibility as a one-off project. New pages, plugins and booking tools can undo earlier fixes. Add an accessibility check to your launch checklist.
  • Relying on an overlay widget alone. The DOJ guidance warns that automated tools need to be used carefully, and a clean report doesn't guarantee accessibility.
  • Ignoring downloads. A beautifully accessible web page that links to an untagged PDF guide still leaves some readers behind.
  • Placeholder text instead of labels. Form fields that only show grey hint text, which disappears when you type, are hard to use for many people. Use visible labels.
  • Fixing pages one by one. Start with shared templates, menus and form styles, so each fix covers the whole site.

Accessibility helps everyone

Many accessibility fixes also make your site better for all visitors and for search engines: clear headings, descriptive link text, readable contrast, fast and simple forms, and text alternatives that help search engines understand images. Frequently asked questions

Does the ADA apply to accounting and finance firm websites?

The Department of Justice says Title III of the ADA covers businesses open to the public and that it has consistently taken the position that the ADA applies to the goods and services they offer online. Whether and how it applies to a particular firm depends on the facts, so take legal advice for your situation.

Which WCAG level should a finance firm website meet?

WCAG has three levels, A, AA and AAA, and Level AA is a common target. The W3C encourages using the latest version, WCAG 2.2, and content that meets WCAG 2.2 also meets WCAG 2.1 and 2.0 because the versions are designed to be backwards compatible.

Are accessibility overlay widgets enough?

Not on their own. The DOJ's guidance says automated checkers and overlays can be helpful tools but need to be used carefully: a clean report doesn't necessarily mean everything is accessible. Pair automated checks with manual testing, such as keyboard-only navigation, zoom and a screen reader on your key forms.

What are the most common accessibility problems on finance firm websites?

Low color contrast, required fields shown only in red, charts and images without text alternatives, forms without proper labels or clear error messages, menus and pop-ups that only work with a mouse, videos without captions, and untagged PDF downloads. Third-party booking and portal tools often need checking too.
