Home Care Agency Website: What to Include and What CQC Requires

Hamid Ali AsadOctober 2026 · 7 min read

The short answer

A home care agency website must show your current CQC rating, its date and a link to your CQC report, on the homepage or main landing pages, within 21 days of publication. It should also list the areas you cover, explain services and fees in plain English, introduce your team, recruit care workers and use short, privacy-friendly forms.

Key takeaways

  • Regulation 20A requires rated providers to show their CQC rating, its date and where to find the report on their website.
  • For domiciliary care, the CQC says to display the rating on the homepage or appropriate landing pages, reachable from the main navigation.
  • New ratings must appear on your website no later than 21 calendar days after the CQC publishes them.
  • Families need clear areas covered, plain-English services, how fees work and an easy way to call.
  • A careers page with one page per vacancy and job posting structured data helps you recruit care workers.

A home care agency website has two audiences, and both arrive worried. Families are trying to arrange care for someone they love, often in a hurry. Care workers are deciding whether your agency is a good place to work. On top of that, your site has a legal job to do: if your service has a CQC rating, you must show it on your website. This guide covers what a home care agency website should include to win enquiries and staff, and what the CQC requires on it. It's written for domiciliary care agencies in England.

The legal part first: displaying your CQC rating

Regulation 20A of the Health and Social Care Act 2008 (Regulated Activities) Regulations 2014 requires providers to display their CQC rating. The CQC's page on Regulation 20A sets out what every website maintained by or on behalf of a rated provider must show:

  • the CQC's website address
  • where on the CQC's website the most recent assessment can be found
  • the most recent rating, made clear which service or premises it relates to

Anything shown on the website must be legible, displayed conspicuously, and show the date each rating was given. The same page says the CQC can prosecute for a breach of this regulation and can move directly to prosecution without first serving a warning notice.

Where the rating must go on a home care website

The CQC's guidance for community-based adult social care, which includes domiciliary care, is specific. You must display your current rating on every website you operate that describes your services. It should be on a permanent page that is:

  • frequently visited by the public
  • conspicuous
  • obvious about which service the rating relates to
  • found through your main website navigation

The guidance says to display it on the main homepage or on appropriate landing pages.

Timing, reviews and inherited ratings

The CQC's main guidance on how providers must display ratings adds a few points worth knowing:

  • You must display new ratings on your website no later than 21 calendar days after the CQC publishes them.
  • You still have to display the rating if you've asked for a review of it, though you may add a note saying so.
  • If you're rated requires improvement or inadequate, the CQC encourages you to explain what you're doing to improve alongside the rating.
  • If you took over a service from another provider and choose to show the previous provider's rating, you must make clear it was awarded to the previous provider.

The CQC provides a website widget that displays your rating automatically, although you can use your own materials. The CQC says using its posters and digital products helps make sure you display all the information the regulation requires. Check the CQC's current guidance before relying on any of this. It isn't legal advice.

What families want to see

Once the legal basics are in place, the website's main job is to help a worried family decide to call you.

The areas you cover, clearly

Families search by place: "home care in [town]" or "carers near me". Put the towns, villages and postcodes you cover on your homepage and on dedicated area pages. If you can't cover an area, say so, so you don't waste each other's time.

Services described in plain English

List the care you provide with a short explanation of each: personal care, medication support, dementia care, companionship, respite, live-in care, end-of-life support. Say what a typical visit involves and the minimum visit length. Families often don't know the jargon, so "help getting washed and dressed in the morning" works better than "personal care packages".

How to get started

Explain the first steps: an initial phone call, a free assessment visit, a care plan agreed with the family, and introductions to the care team. Knowing what happens next makes it easier to pick up the phone.

Fees, or at least how fees work

Many agencies don't publish prices. You don't have to, but explaining how charges work helps. Are visits charged by the hour or in shorter blocks? Are evening, weekend and bank holiday rates different? Do you work with council-funded and self-funded clients? Being open about this builds trust.

Real people

Show your registered manager and office team by name, with real photos. Families want to know who they'll be dealing with. If you use testimonials, use genuine ones with permission, and never invent them.

Easy contact

Make your phone number large and clickable on mobile, and show your office hours and out-of-hours number. A short enquiry form helps families who would rather not call. Ask only for what you need: their name, a contact number, the area and the type of help needed.

Recruiting care workers through your website

Staffing can limit an agency's growth as much as finding clients. Your website should work as a recruitment tool, not just a brochure.

  • A proper careers page. Explain pay, how rotas work, mileage or travel time, training, uniforms and the support available. Care workers compare agencies, and specifics win.
  • One page per vacancy. Google's job posting documentation says adding job posting structured data to a page makes it eligible for Google's job search experience. It also says that data must only be on a page dedicated to a single job, not a list of vacancies. Closed jobs must be expired or removed promptly.
  • A quick application. Applicants often use a phone. Let them start with their name, phone number, postcode and whether they drive. You can collect the rest at interview.

I cover careers pages in more depth in my guide to recruiting through your own website. It's written for engineering firms, but the structure and the Google job posting rules apply to care agencies just the same.

Data protection on care websites

Enquiry forms on a care website often collect sensitive information about someone's health. Keep forms short and avoid asking for medical details online; collect those at the assessment visit instead.

The ICO's guidance on the right to be informed says you must give people privacy information when you collect their data. That includes why you're collecting it, your lawful basis, how long you keep it and who you share it with, in clear, plain language. A link to your privacy notice next to every form is a simple way to do this.

Frequently asked questions

Do home care agencies have to show their CQC rating on their website?

Yes, if the service has a CQC rating. Regulation 20A requires every website maintained by or for a rated provider to show the CQC's website address, where the latest assessment can be found and the most recent rating with its date. The CQC says domiciliary care agencies should display it on the homepage or appropriate landing pages.

How quickly must a new CQC rating appear on our website?

The CQC's display guidance says you must display your ratings on posters at premises and on websites no later than 21 calendar days after the CQC publishes them. You must still display the rating if you've asked for a review, though you can add a note explaining that a review has been requested.

What should a domiciliary care website include?

Your CQC rating, the towns and postcodes you cover, plain-English descriptions of your services, how getting started works, how your fees work, your registered manager and team, a clickable phone number with a short enquiry form, a careers section for care workers and a privacy notice linked from every form.

How can a care agency recruit carers through its website?

Create a careers page that explains pay, rotas, travel, training and support, give each vacancy its own page with Google's job posting structured data, remove closed jobs promptly, and keep the first application step short enough to complete on a phone, such as name, phone number, postcode and whether they drive.
