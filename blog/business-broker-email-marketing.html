Business Broker Email Marketing: Stay in Touch With Future Sellers

Hamid Ali AsadNovember 2026 · 6 min read

The short answer

Business broker email marketing keeps you in touch with owners who aren't ready to sell. Offer a specific resource such as a sale-preparation checklist, place sign-up forms on valuation and seller pages, keep seller and buyer lists separate, send short, useful emails on a regular schedule, and follow CAN-SPAM rules on headers, addresses and opt-outs.

Key takeaways

  • Many owners who visit a broker's site aren't ready to sell, so email keeps you in touch until they are.
  • A specific offer, such as a sale-preparation checklist, gives future sellers a reason to subscribe.
  • Keep buyer and seller lists separate so each group gets only relevant emails.
  • The FTC says CAN-SPAM covers all commercial email, with no exception for business-to-business messages.
  • CAN-SPAM requires accurate headers, a postal address, a clear opt-out and honoring opt-outs within 10 business days.

Most business owners who visit a broker's website aren't ready to sell. They're curious, planning a few years ahead or waiting for the right moment. If your site's only offer is "list your business with us", they leave and you never hear from them again. Business broker email marketing fixes that. A short, useful newsletter keeps you in front of future sellers until they're ready, so that when the time comes, you're the broker they already know and trust. This guide covers how to build an email list from your website, what to send, and the US rules your emails must follow.

Why email suits business brokers

A business sale is a long, considered decision. An owner might read your valuation page today and not pick up the phone for another year or two. In between, they'll talk to their accountant, read about exit planning and possibly hear from other brokers.

Email lets you stay in touch through that whole period without being pushy. A monthly or quarterly note with something genuinely useful, such as how to prepare a business for sale or what buyers in their sector look for, keeps your name in front of them and shows you know your market.

Building the list from your website

Offer something worth subscribing for

"Subscribe to our newsletter" gives an owner little reason to sign up. Offer something specific that a future seller wants:

  • a checklist for preparing a business for sale
  • a short guide to how buyers value businesses in their sector
  • a periodic market update for their industry or region
  • an exit planning timeline for owners one to three years from selling

Put sign-up forms where future sellers are

  • on your valuation page, as an alternative for owners not ready to talk
  • at the end of every article aimed at sellers
  • on sector pages, offering sector-specific updates
  • on your "selling your business" page

My guide to business broker valuation pages covers how to turn "What's my business worth?" visitors into conversations; email gives you a way to keep the rest.

Ask for very little

An email address, a first name and, optionally, the type of business is enough. Extra fields can put people off, and they add to the data you're responsible for. The UK ICO's guidance on data minimisation is a useful standard wherever you are: collect what you need for your purpose, not information that might be useful someday.

Explain what they're signing up for

Say what you'll send and how often: "A short email once a month with practical advice for owners thinking about selling. Unsubscribe any time." Link to your privacy policy. The ICO's guidance on the right to be informed says people should get privacy information at the time you collect their data, which a short note under the form does.

What to send future sellers

Keep emails short, practical and focused on the owner, not on your listings. A simple mix:

Content Example Why it works
Preparation tips "Five things buyers check in your financials" Helps owners and shows expertise
Market notes What's happening in deals in their sector or region Keeps them informed
Answers to common questions "How long does selling a business usually take at our firm?" Builds trust
Recently sold examples Anonymized deals, used with permission Proof you close deals
A gentle next step "Want a confidential chat about timing?" Opens the door without pressure

Send on a predictable schedule. Monthly or quarterly are both reasonable; pick what you can sustain. Consistency matters more than frequency.

Separate your buyer and seller lists

Buyers and sellers want different things. Buyers want new listings and deal alerts; owners thinking of selling want preparation advice. Use separate lists or tags from the start so each group only gets relevant emails. Sending listing alerts to owners, or exit planning advice to buyers, is a quick way to lose subscribers.

The US rules: CAN-SPAM

The FTC's CAN-SPAM Act compliance guide explains that the law covers all commercial email, defined as messages whose primary purpose is the commercial advertisement or promotion of a commercial product or service. The FTC is clear that the law makes no exception for business-to-business email.

The guide's main requirements include:

  • Accurate header information. "From", "To", "Reply-To" and routing information must be accurate and identify who sent the message.
  • No deceptive subject lines. The subject must accurately reflect the content.
  • Identify the message as an ad. You have leeway in how you do this, but it must be clear and conspicuous.
  • Include your postal address. A valid physical postal address, a registered P.O. box or a registered private mailbox.
  • Explain how to opt out. A clear, easy way to stop marketing emails, such as an unsubscribe link.
  • Honor opt-outs promptly. Opt-out requests must be honored within 10 business days, and you can't charge a fee or ask for more than an email address to do so. Once someone opts out, you can't sell or transfer their address.
  • You're responsible for your provider. Hiring an email marketing company doesn't transfer your legal responsibility.

The FTC says each separate email in violation can be subject to penalties of up to $53,088, so it's worth getting right. Many email platforms add an unsubscribe link and address footer for you, but check your templates.

UK brokers: the UK has its own rules on electronic marketing, covered in the ICO's direct marketing guidance. Check the current ICO guidance before emailing UK contacts. None of this is legal advice.

Before and after: a newsletter email

Here's an invented example.

Before: Subject: "HOT NEW LISTINGS!!!" Body: a list of eight businesses for sale, sent to everyone who ever filled in a form.

After: Subject: "Three things buyers check first in a services business". Body: three short, practical points, a link to a longer article, one anonymized recently-sold example used with permission, and a closing line: "Thinking about timing? Reply to this email for a confidential chat." Footer with your postal address and an unsubscribe link. Sent only to the owners list.

The second email is accurate, useful and clearly from a real firm, and it follows CAN-SPAM's basics.

Frequently asked questions

Do business brokers need an email newsletter?

It helps. Many owners who visit a broker's website are months or years from selling. A short, useful newsletter with preparation tips, market notes and anonymized recent sales keeps your name in front of them, so you're the broker they already know when they're ready to talk about listing.

Does CAN-SPAM apply to business-to-business emails?

Yes. The FTC's compliance guide says the CAN-SPAM Act covers all commercial email and makes no exception for business-to-business email. Messages must have accurate header information, non-deceptive subject lines, identification as an ad, a valid postal address and a clear way to opt out, with opt-outs honored within 10 business days.

What should a business broker send to future sellers?

Practical, owner-focused content: tips on preparing a business for sale, what buyers check in financials, market notes for their sector or region, answers to common questions, anonymized recent sales used with permission, and an occasional gentle invitation to a confidential chat. Keep it short and avoid sending buyer-style listing alerts.

How should brokers collect email sign-ups on their website?

Offer something specific, such as a sale-preparation checklist or exit planning timeline, and place forms on valuation, seller and sector pages and at the end of seller articles. Ask only for an email address and first name, explain what you'll send and how often, and link to your privacy policy.
