How to Recruit Structural Engineers Through Your Own Website

Hamid Ali AsadOctober 2026 · 6 min read

The short answer

To recruit structural engineers through your website, build a careers page that shows the work, the people and how you support chartership or registration. Publish salary and flexibility details, give each vacancy its own page with Google's job posting structured data, remove closed roles promptly, and make applying quick on a phone.

Key takeaways

  • Your careers page is the one place you control the whole story candidates hear about your firm.
  • Specific support for chartership, such as paid fees and mentoring, is more convincing than general promises.
  • Google's job posting structured data makes vacancy pages eligible for its job search, but each job needs its own page.
  • Closed vacancies must be expired or removed, or Google may take manual action.
  • In Victoria, check how registration and direct supervision apply before you describe graduate roles.

If you're working out how to recruit structural engineers without paying an agency fee for every hire, start with your own website. Engineers often look at a firm's site before they apply, or before they agree to a recruiter's introduction. They look at the projects, the people and whether you'll support them towards chartership or registration. If your careers page is a single line saying "send your CV to info@", you're relying on agencies and job boards to do the persuading for you. This guide covers what to put on the page, how to get your vacancies into Google's job search, and the mistakes that put good candidates off.

Why your website matters when you recruit structural engineers

Agencies and job boards are useful, but they present every firm the same way: a job title, a salary band and a location. Your website is the only place where you control the whole story. That includes the type of work, who the candidate would be working with, and how you develop people.

It also matters for Google. Google's own job posting documentation says that adding job posting structured data to your vacancy pages makes them eligible to appear in its job search experience. Job seekers can filter by location and job title there, so a local graduate searching "graduate structural engineer" plus your town has a way to find your firm directly.

What a good engineering careers page includes

The kind of work they'll actually do

Engineers want to know whether they'll spend their days on residential extensions, commercial steel frames, remedial work, infrastructure or a mix. Say so plainly, and link to a few relevant projects in your portfolio. A short line on each project ("steel frame for a three-storey mixed-use building, designed in-house") tells a candidate more than a paragraph about "exciting opportunities".

Who they'll work with and learn from

Introduce the people a new starter would sit with, especially senior or chartered engineers. The Engineering Council's guidance for employers suggests displaying employees' professional titles and post-nominal letters so their achievement is recognised. On a careers page, that also shows candidates there are people around who have done it and can mentor them.

How you support chartership and registration

For many graduate and mid-level engineers, the path to chartership is an important factor in choosing an employer. The same Engineering Council page lists practical ways employers can support registration. These include paying membership and registration fees, sponsoring education, offering mentoring, and getting a structured graduate scheme approved or accredited against the professional standards. It notes that paying fees "is clear evidence that you place real value on the professional development of your employees".

If you do any of these things, put them on your careers page in specific terms. "We pay your institution membership and give you a mentor who is already chartered" is far more convincing than "we support professional development".

Registration requirements, explained honestly

In Australia, registration can affect who you can hire into which role. In Victoria, for example, Consumer Affairs Victoria explains that people providing professional engineering services in structural or civil engineering must be registered. There's an exemption for work done under the direct supervision of a registered professional engineer.

That matters for graduates: tell them how supervision works at your firm and how you'll help them work towards registration. Check the current rules with the regulator in each state you work in before you write this section. This isn't legal advice.

Salary, hours and flexibility

Candidates compare firms side by side. If you can publish a salary range, do. If you offer hybrid working, a nine-day fortnight or study leave, say so clearly. Vague phrases like "competitive package" give people no reason to choose you over the firm whose page lists the details.

A simple way to apply

Ask for a CV and a short note, and make it easy to send them from a phone. Long application forms that repeat what's in the CV lose people. Tell candidates what happens next and roughly how long it takes, then stick to it.

Getting your vacancies into Google's job search

This is the part most engineering firms miss. Google's job search experience reads structured data on your vacancy pages. According to Google's documentation, the required properties are:

  • title: the job title
  • description: the full description, including responsibilities, qualifications, skills, working hours, education and experience requirements
  • datePosted: when you posted the job
  • hiringOrganization: your company name
  • jobLocation: where the job is based

Recommended properties include baseSalary, employmentType, validThrough (the closing date) and jobLocationType for remote roles.

A few rules from the same page are easy to break:

  • One job per page. Google says JobPosting structured data must only be on a page dedicated to a single job, not on a page that lists several vacancies. So give every role its own page, with a jobs list page linking to them.
  • Remove closed jobs promptly. Jobs that are no longer open must be expired by setting a past validThrough date, removing the page, or removing the structured data. Google warns that not doing so may lead to a manual action.
  • Salary must come from you. Google notes that only employers can provide baseSalary, and it should be the actual salary, not an estimate.

On WordPress this is straightforward to set up. A custom "Vacancies" post type with fields for location, salary, contract type and closing date can generate the structured data automatically. Your team then fills in a form and never touches code.

Before and after: a vacancy intro

Here's an invented example of a typical vacancy intro, rewritten.

Before: "We are looking for a talented structural engineer to join our dynamic team. Competitive salary. Send your CV to info@."

After: "We're hiring a graduate structural engineer for our Brisbane office. You'll work on residential extensions, small commercial buildings and remedial jobs, with a chartered engineer reviewing your designs. We pay your institution membership and give you study time towards chartership. Salary range and full details below. Apply with your CV and a short note; we reply to everyone within two weeks."

The second version answers the questions a candidate would otherwise have to ask, and it reads like a real firm rather than a template.

Common mistakes that cost you applicants

  • Old vacancies left live. A role that closed months ago makes the firm look disorganised, and Google's rules require closed jobs to be expired.
  • All jobs on one page. It's tidy, but it stops each role being eligible for Google's job search.
  • No projects or people. Without them, your careers page looks the same as every agency listing.
  • A slow, clunky site on mobile. Candidates often browse vacancies on their phone. My Core Web Vitals guide covers what to fix on WordPress.

Frequently asked questions

How do I get my engineering jobs to show on Google?

Give each vacancy its own page and add JobPosting structured data. Google lists title, description, datePosted, hiringOrganization and jobLocation as required properties, with salary, employment type and closing date recommended. Pages that list several jobs shouldn't carry the structured data. Remove or expire jobs once they close.

What should a structural engineering firm put on its careers page?

Describe the type of work, link to real projects, introduce the senior and chartered engineers a new starter would learn from, and explain how you support chartership or registration. Add salary ranges, hours and flexibility, list each vacancy on its own page, and make it quick to apply with a CV and a short note.

Can graduates work as structural engineers in Victoria without being registered?

Consumer Affairs Victoria explains that providing professional engineering services in structural engineering requires registration, but there is an exemption for work done under the direct supervision of a registered professional engineer. Check the current guidance from Consumer Affairs Victoria for how this applies to your firm and roles.

Is it worth paying engineers' institution fees to attract staff?

The Engineering Council suggests paying employees' membership and registration fees as one way to support professional registration, and says the investment may be repaid through loyalty and retention. If you offer it, state it clearly on your careers page, because it answers a question many candidates have about employers.
