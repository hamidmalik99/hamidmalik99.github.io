Service Area Pages for Engineers: Rank Locally Without Doorways

Hamid Ali AsadNovember 2026 · 6 min read

The short answer

Service area pages for engineers should each give genuine local value: real projects in that area, local conditions and common job types, the approvals context, the local team and lead times, and a link to your fee proposal form. Build pages only for core areas, group smaller places regionally, link them through a hub page and avoid city-list paragraphs.

Key takeaways

  • Google's spam policies treat near-identical city pages that funnel users to one page as doorway abuse.
  • Google lists blocks of text naming cities you want to rank for as an example of keyword stuffing.
  • Each area page needs genuine local content, ideally real projects you've completed there.
  • Start with a few core areas and group smaller suburbs or towns into regional pages.
  • Link area pages through a hub and to services and projects, forming a clear, browseable hierarchy.

Most structural and civil engineering consultancies serve a patch of suburbs or towns, and most clients search with a place name: "structural engineer Footscray", "civil engineer Reading". Service area pages for engineers are how you show up for those searches. Done well, each page gives a builder or homeowner real reasons to trust you in their area. Done badly, with the same text copied twenty times and the suburb name swapped, they look like spam to Google and to readers. This guide covers what Google's policies say, what a good engineering area page contains, how many to build and how to link them.

What Google says about location pages

Google's spam policies describe "doorway abuse" as sites or pages created to rank for specific, similar search queries that lead users to intermediate pages that aren't as useful as the final destination. The examples Google lists include:

  • having multiple domain names or pages targeted at specific regions or cities that funnel users to one page
  • creating substantially similar pages that are closer to search results than a clearly defined, browseable hierarchy

The same page describes "scaled content abuse": many pages generated mainly to manipulate rankings rather than help users, typically unoriginal content with little value, however it's created, including with AI tools.

It also lists "keyword stuffing", and one of Google's own examples is blocks of text that list cities and regions a web page is trying to rank for. The long "we serve Suburb A, Suburb B, Suburb C…" paragraph at the bottom of many engineering websites is exactly that.

None of this means area pages are banned. It means each one has to be genuinely useful to someone in that area.

What a good engineering area page contains

Think of each area page as a short local briefing for a builder, architect or homeowner there.

Local work you've actually done

The strongest content is real projects in or near that area, with client permission: "Steel frame for a rear extension in [suburb]", "Retaining wall design on a sloping block in [town]". Link each one to its full project page. My guide to engineering project portfolios covers how to write those.

Local conditions and common jobs

Explain what's typical in that area from an engineering point of view, based on your own experience: the housing stock, the kinds of renovations you see, site access issues, slopes, or ground conditions you commonly design for. Keep it factual and specific to your work there. If you don't have genuine local knowledge of an area, that's a sign it shouldn't have its own page yet.

The local approvals context

Name the council, building control body or certifiers you commonly work with in that area, and explain in general terms how your documents fit into their process. Check details with the authority rather than stating requirements from memory.

Practical details

Who covers that area from your team, typical lead times for inspections there, and whether you charge travel for outlying parts of it.

A clear next step

Link to your fee proposal form with the area pre-selected if you can. My guide to the structural engineer enquiry form covers what that form should ask.

How many area pages should you build?

Fewer, better pages beat dozens of thin ones. A practical approach:

  1. Start with your core areas, the three to eight places where you do the most work and have real projects to show.
  2. Group smaller suburbs or villages into a regional page, such as "Inner West" or "North Somerset", instead of a page each.
  3. Add a new page only when you have something specific to say: projects, local conditions or a local team member.

Your Google Business Profile should reflect the same reality. Google's guidelines for representing your business say service-area businesses should set a designated service area, and that the overall service area generally shouldn't extend further than about two hours' driving time from where you're based. Keep your website's area pages consistent with that.

Linking area pages together

Google's spam policy contrasts doorway pages with "a clearly defined, browseable hierarchy". Build that hierarchy on purpose:

  • An "Areas we cover" hub page, linked from your main navigation or footer, listing each area page with a one-line summary.
  • Each area page links to relevant service pages, such as inspections, extensions or retaining walls, and to local projects.
  • Each project page links back to its area page.
  • Neighbouring areas link to each other where it helps a reader, not as a block of links at the bottom.

Before and after: an area page opening

Here's an invented example of the same page written two ways.

Before: "We are the leading structural engineers in [Suburb]. Our [Suburb] structural engineers provide structural engineering services to [Suburb] and surrounding areas including [list of 30 suburbs]."

After: "We've designed extensions, wall removals and retaining walls across [Suburb] for builders and homeowners for several years. Many homes here are older weatherboard houses on narrow blocks, so most of our local work involves opening up internal walls and designing new footings beside existing ones. Recent local projects include a two-storey rear extension and a retaining wall on a sloping corner block (see below). Inspections in [Suburb] are usually booked within a week."

The second version is specific, readable and only possible for a firm that really works there, which is exactly what Google and clients are looking for. Replace every detail with your own; don't copy these claims.

Common area page mistakes

  • Swapping the suburb name in a template. If the only difference between two pages is the place name, they're the "substantially similar pages" Google describes.
  • Generating pages in bulk with AI. Google's scaled content policy applies however the content is created, so dozens of generated pages with no real local detail are a risk.
  • Pages for places you rarely work. If you can't name a project or a local issue, the page has nothing useful to say. Cover those places on a regional page instead.
  • Orphaned pages. Area pages that aren't linked from your hub, services or projects are hard for visitors and Google to find.
  • Inconsistent service areas. If your website covers places your Business Profile doesn't, or the reverse, tidy one or the other.

Frequently asked questions

Are location pages bad for SEO?

Not in themselves. Google's spam policies target doorway abuse, such as many substantially similar pages for different cities that funnel users to one page, and scaled content created mainly to manipulate rankings. Location pages with genuine local content, such as real projects and local conditions, are useful to readers and fit within Google's guidance.

How many service area pages should an engineering firm have?

Only as many as you can fill with genuine local content. Start with the three to eight areas where you do most of your work and have projects to show, group smaller suburbs or villages into regional pages, and add new pages only when you have something specific to say about an area.

Should I list all the suburbs I serve on my website?

Avoid long paragraphs listing suburbs or towns. Google's spam policies give blocks of text listing cities and regions a page is trying to rank for as an example of keyword stuffing. Instead, create a hub page linking to your core area pages and set your service area in your Google Business Profile.

What should an engineering service area page include?

Real local projects shared with client permission, the typical local conditions and job types you handle there, the local approvals context, who covers the area and typical lead times, any travel charges, links to relevant service and project pages, and a clear link to your fee proposal form.
