Local SEO for Home Care Agencies: Get Found by Families Nearby

Hamid Ali AsadNovember 2026 · 6 min read

The short answer

Local SEO for home care agencies starts with a Google Business Profile set up as a service-area business, using your real name, a realistic service area and few categories. Ask families for reviews without incentives, follow CMA guidance if you publish reviews, and back the profile with genuine area pages and your CQC rating on your website.

Key takeaways

  • Google says local results are mainly based on relevance, distance and prominence, and better rankings can't be bought.
  • Domiciliary care agencies are service-area businesses and should hide the address if clients don't visit the office.
  • Your profile name must be your real business name; adding keywords can get it suspended.
  • Google prohibits incentives for reviews, and the CMA expects businesses publishing reviews to have a fake reviews policy.
  • Genuine area pages and a correctly displayed CQC rating support your local visibility and win families' trust.

When a family needs home care, they usually start with a search like "home care near me" or "carers in [town]". The agencies that appear in Google's map results and the first few listings get the calls. Local SEO for home care agencies is about making sure yours is one of them, with a Google Business Profile that follows the rules, reviews that build trust, and a website that backs it all up. This guide covers what Google says about local rankings, how to set up a profile for a domiciliary care agency, how to handle reviews under UK rules, and what your website needs to support it. It's written for agencies in England.

How Google decides which agencies appear

Google's guide to improving your local ranking says local results are mainly based on three things:

  • Relevance: how well your Business Profile matches what someone searched for. Complete, detailed business information helps.
  • Distance: how far each business is from the person searching, or the location they searched for.
  • Prominence: how well known your business is. Google says this is also based on information such as how many websites link to you and how many reviews you have, and that more reviews and positive ratings can help.

The same page says there's no way to request or pay for a better local ranking. You earn it.

Setting up your Google Business Profile as a home care agency

Google's guidelines for representing your business cover a few points that matter for domiciliary care.

Your agency is a service-area business

Your carers visit people in their own homes. Google says service-area businesses should have one profile for the central office with a designated service area. If clients don't visit your office, Google says you should hide your address. If they do visit during staffed hours, you can show the address and still set a service area.

Google also says a service area generally shouldn't extend further than about two hours' driving time from where you're based. In practice, set it to the towns and postcodes you genuinely cover.

Branches

Google says that if you have different locations with separate service areas and separate staff at each, you can have a profile for each location. A virtual office isn't eligible, and a co-working desk only counts if it has signage, receives customers and is staffed during opening hours.

Use your real name

Your profile name must match your real-world business name, as used on your signage, website and stationery. Google says adding unnecessary information, such as service or location keywords, isn't permitted and could get your profile suspended. For example, "[Agency name] Home Care" is fine; "[Agency name] Home Care – Best Carers in [Town] 24/7" is not.

Categories, hours and contact details

Choose the fewest categories that describe your core business; Google asks for categories that complete the sentence "this business IS a…". Add your office hours, a local phone number you control and your website. If you offer an out-of-hours line, mention it in your description and on your website.

Reviews: ask every family, within the rules

Reviews feed directly into prominence, and families read them closely.

Google's tips to get more reviews say you can ask customers to leave reviews using a Google link or QR code, and encourage replying to reviews. The same page says offering incentives, such as discounts, in exchange for reviews, or to change or remove negative ones, is fake engagement and strictly prohibited.

UK consumer law adds its own expectations. The CMA's short guide for businesses publishing consumer reviews says traders who publish reviews, including on their own website, should have a published policy that prohibits fake reviews and sets out their approach to incentivised reviews, and must take reasonable and proportionate steps to prevent and remove banned content. If you show reviews on your site, follow this guidance.

Practical approach for a care agency:

  • Ask families for feedback at natural points, such as a care review or after the first few weeks of a new package.
  • Make it easy with a link or QR code, and accept that not everyone will want to post publicly.
  • Reply to every review briefly and professionally. Never share personal or care details in a reply.
  • Respond to negative reviews calmly and offer to talk privately.

What your website needs to support local SEO

Your Business Profile gets you into the map results; your website backs it up and turns the visit into a call.

Pages for the areas you cover

Create a page for each main town or area you serve, with genuine local detail: the villages and postcodes covered, how far ahead you can usually start a package there, and the local team. Avoid copying the same page and swapping the town name.

Your CQC rating, displayed correctly

If your service has a CQC rating, you must display it on your website. The CQC's guidance on displaying ratings for community-based adult social care says to show your current rating on every website you operate that describes your services, on a permanent page that is conspicuous, clear about which service it relates to and reachable from your main navigation, such as your homepage or appropriate landing pages. It's also one of the first things a family looks for.

Consistent details

Use the same business name, phone number and service areas on your website as on your Business Profile, so families and Google see a consistent picture.

Easy contact on mobile

Many families will find you on a phone. Make the phone number large and clickable, show office hours, and keep the enquiry form short.

Frequently asked questions

How do home care agencies show up in Google Maps?

Set up a Google Business Profile as a service-area business with your real business name, the fewest accurate categories, office hours, a local phone number and your website. Set a realistic service area, hide your address if clients don't visit, and build reviews. Google says local results depend on relevance, distance and prominence.

Should a domiciliary care agency hide its address on Google?

Google's guidelines say service-area businesses that serve customers at their locations should hide their address if customers don't visit. If families do visit your office during staffed hours, you can show the address and still set a service area. Virtual offices aren't eligible for a Business Profile.

Can care agencies ask families for Google reviews?

Yes. Google says you can ask customers to leave reviews using a link or QR code, but offering incentives in exchange for reviews is fake engagement and strictly prohibited. If you publish reviews on your own website, the CMA expects a policy prohibiting fake reviews and reasonable steps to prevent and remove them.

Do home care agencies need location pages on their website?

Pages for the main towns or areas you serve help, as long as each has genuine local detail such as the villages and postcodes covered, typical start times for new packages and the local team. Avoid near-identical pages with only the town name changed, and link each page from your main navigation.
