Selling Lifting Equipment Online: What Your Product Pages Need

Hamid Ali AsadOctober 2026 · 7 min read

The short answer

To sell lifting equipment online, each product page should show the safe working load for every configuration, a consistent specification table, variants for capacity and length, downloadable instructions and data sheets, and confirmation that a Declaration of Conformity is supplied. Add stock and delivery details, trade account features and the right Product structured data.

Key takeaways

  • HSE says all lifting equipment and accessories must be clearly marked with their safe working load, including for each configuration.
  • Most lifting equipment must be accompanied by a Declaration of Conformity, which the user should keep, so say on the page that it's supplied.
  • A consistent specification table and variant selector make it easy for trade buyers to match products to a spec.
  • Google distinguishes merchant listings for buyable products from product snippets for quote-only pages, so mark up each correctly.
  • Certificate downloads, trade pricing, quote requests and quick reorder suit how industrial customers buy.

Selling lifting equipment online is different from selling most products. A buyer choosing a chain block, a set of slings or a beam clamp isn't browsing; they need the right rating, the right length and the right paperwork for an inspection or a site audit. If your product pages leave out the safe working load or the Declaration of Conformity, the buyer either rings you to check, or buys from a supplier whose page answered the question. This guide covers what product pages for lifting equipment should include, the UK rules that shape that information, and how to set up a WooCommerce store so trade buyers can order without friction.

Why lifting equipment product pages need more than a photo and a price

Trade buyers of lifting equipment usually work to a specification. A site manager, maintenance engineer or procurement officer needs to confirm capacity, dimensions, standards and documentation before ordering, and often needs to keep records for the equipment once it arrives.

That's partly because of the law the buyer works under. The HSE's LOLER overview explains that the Lifting Operations and Lifting Equipment Regulations 1998 place duties on people and companies who own, operate or have control over lifting equipment. LOLER requires lifting equipment to be fit for purpose, appropriate for the task and suitably marked, and in many cases subject to statutory periodic thorough examination. Your customers carry those duties, so your product pages should give them the information they need to meet them.

What the rules say about marking and documentation

This section explains what the regulations say so your pages reflect them accurately. It isn't legal advice; check the current HSE guidance for your products.

Safe working load

The HSE says all lifting equipment, including accessories, must be clearly marked to show its safe working load (SWL), the maximum load it can safely lift. Where the SWL depends on configuration, the information must reflect all potential configurations. Accessories must also be marked with any characteristics that might affect safe use, such as their weight where it's significant.

On your website, that means the SWL (or working load limit) should be prominent on every product page, not buried in a PDF. If a product has different ratings in different configurations, such as a sling used straight, choked or in a basket, show each one in a clear table.

Declaration of Conformity

The same HSE page explains that most lifting equipment and lifting accessories fall within the scope of the Supply of Machinery (Safety) Regulations. Such equipment must have been through appropriate conformity assessment, be labelled and be accompanied by a Declaration of Conformity (DoC) before being placed on the market or brought into use. This includes manually operated equipment such as chain blocks.

The HSE adds that the DoC is an important document that the user should keep. It may also avoid the need for an initial thorough examination before first use in some cases, where the equipment's safety doesn't depend on how it's installed or assembled.

So tell buyers on the product page that each item is supplied with its Declaration of Conformity, and explain how they can get a copy again if it's lost. A "download your certificates" area in the customer account, organised by order and serial number, is a practical way to do this.

Instructions and responsibilities across the supply chain

The HSE's guidance on UK law on the design and supply of products explains that new machinery and other products in scope of the Supply of Machinery (Safety) Regulations, including lifting accessories, chains, rope and webbing, must be supplied with comprehensive instructions in English and a Declaration of Conformity. The page also explains that distributors who market products under their own name are treated as "responsible persons" under those regulations. Others in the supply chain, including intermediate suppliers and those hiring out equipment for use at work, are covered by section 6 of the Health and Safety at Work etc Act 1974, which includes providing information and instructions on safe use.

Making user instructions downloadable from each product page is good practice. If you sell under your own brand, check with your advisers which obligations apply to you.

What every lifting equipment product page should include

A specification table, not a paragraph

Put the facts buyers compare into a consistent table on every product: working load limit, lifting height or length, dimensions, weight, materials, grade, relevant standards and any configuration ratings. Keep the same fields in the same order across the range so buyers can compare items quickly.

Variants that are easy to choose

Lifting gear often comes in many capacities and lengths. Use product variants so a buyer can pick "2 tonne, 3 m" from one page instead of hunting through dozens of near-identical products. Show the SWL and price for the selected variant as it changes.

Documents next to the product

Offer the user instructions, data sheet and an example DoC as downloads on the page. State clearly what paperwork ships with the item.

Clear availability and delivery

Trade buyers often need equipment for a specific job. Show stock status, dispatch cut-off times and delivery options, including next-day and collection if you offer them.

Inspection, testing and hire, if you offer them

If you also provide thorough examinations, test certificates or hire, link to those services from relevant products. A buyer looking at a chain hoist may also need its examination arranged.

Getting your products into Google

Google's introduction to Product structured data explains that adding structured data to product pages can let product information, such as price and availability, appear in richer ways in Google Search, including Google Images and Lens. It distinguishes between two types:

  • Merchant listings, for pages where customers can buy the product from you
  • Product snippets, for product pages where people can't buy directly

That matters for lifting suppliers, because many have a mix: some items with "add to basket" and others that are quote-only. Use the right type for each. Google also recommends product variant markup for products that come in variations, and notes that providing both on-page structured data and a Google Merchant Center feed gives the widest eligibility.

WooCommerce and SEO plugins can generate much of this markup, but check it with Google's Rich Results Test, especially for variable products and quote-only items.

A before and after example

Here's an invented example of a typical product description, rewritten.

Before: "High quality chain block. Robust construction. Ideal for workshops and sites. Call for details."

After: "Manual chain block, 2 tonne working load limit, available in 3 m, 6 m and 10 m lift heights. Supplied with user instructions and a Declaration of Conformity. Specification table, data sheet and example DoC available to download below. In stock: order by 2pm for next working day delivery. Thorough examination service available."

The second version answers the buyer's questions before they need to pick up the phone, and gives Google far more to understand.

Frequently asked questions

What information should a lifting equipment product page include?

The working load limit for each configuration, a consistent specification table covering dimensions, weight, materials, grade and standards, variant options for capacity and length, downloadable instructions and data sheets, confirmation that a Declaration of Conformity is supplied, stock status and delivery options, plus links to inspection or hire services if you offer them.

Does lifting equipment need a Declaration of Conformity?

The HSE explains that most lifting equipment and lifting accessories fall under the Supply of Machinery (Safety) Regulations and must be accompanied by a Declaration of Conformity before being placed on the market or brought into use, including manually operated items such as chain blocks. Check the current HSE guidance for your specific products.

Can you sell lifting equipment on WooCommerce?

Yes. WooCommerce handles variable products for capacities and lengths, and can be extended with trade accounts, account pricing, quote requests, quick reorder and certificate downloads. Pair it with clear specification tables and checked Product structured data so items can appear with details such as price and availability in Google.

What does safe working load mean for lifting equipment?

The HSE describes the safe working load (SWL) as the maximum load the equipment can safely lift, and says all lifting equipment, including accessories, must be clearly marked with it. Where the SWL depends on configuration, the information must cover every configuration, so show each rating clearly on the product page.
