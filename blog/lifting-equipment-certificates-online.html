Lifting Equipment Certificates Online: Build a Customer Portal

Hamid Ali AsadOctober 2026 · 6 min read

The short answer

A lifting equipment certificate portal gives trade customers a secure login where every Declaration of Conformity, thorough examination report and inspection record is stored against each item's serial number. Show next due dates, send reminders, allow search and export, and keep documents read-only, since HSE says records should be protected from unauthorised alteration.

Key takeaways

  • HSE says records should be kept of all thorough examinations, inspections and Declarations of Conformity for lifting equipment.
  • Records don't have to be paper, but users must be able to produce a written copy, and records should be protected from alteration.
  • Thorough examination reports include the next due date, so a portal can show status and send reminders.
  • Organise the portal by equipment item and serial number, with search, PDF download and register export.
  • WooCommerce customer accounts can be extended into a certificate portal with private document storage.

If you supply, hire out or inspect lifting equipment, your customers ask for the same documents again and again: the Declaration of Conformity for a new sling, last year's thorough examination report for a chain block, the certificate an auditor wants to see this afternoon. Putting lifting equipment certificates online, in a secure customer area of your website, saves your office hours of searching and emailing and gives trade customers a reason to keep ordering from you. This guide covers what records customers need, what the HSE says about keeping them, and how to build a certificate portal into a WordPress or WooCommerce site.

Why customers need their certificates quickly

Your customers carry legal duties for the lifting equipment they use. The HSE's guidance on thorough examinations and inspections of lifting equipment explains that records should be kept of all thorough examinations and inspections, and of the Declarations of Conformity for all lifting equipment and lifting accessories.

The same page says those records don't need to be kept in hard copy, but the user should be able to provide a written copy when necessary, for example when the enforcing authority asks or when equipment leaves their business through hire, use elsewhere or second-hand sale. The records should also be protected from unauthorised alteration.

When a site manager can't find a certificate, they ring you. If you can give them a login where every document is waiting, you've solved their problem and made yourself hard to replace.

What records a certificate portal should hold

Declarations of Conformity

The HSE's LOLER overview explains that most lifting equipment and accessories must be accompanied by a Declaration of Conformity before being placed on the market or brought into use, and that the user should keep it. It may also avoid the need for an initial thorough examination before first use, where safety doesn't depend on installation or assembly.

The thorough examination page adds that this applies where the Declaration of Conformity is less than one year old and the equipment wasn't assembled on site. That makes the date on each DoC important, so show it clearly in the portal.

Reports of thorough examination

If you also carry out thorough examinations, the reports belong in the portal. The HSE explains that a thorough examination is a systematic and detailed examination by a competent person, who must complete a written report. That report must contain the information required by LOLER Schedule 1, including:

  • the examination date
  • the date the next thorough examination is due
  • any defects found which are, or could become, a danger to people

The HSE notes there's no longer a defined format for the report, provided all 11 items listed in Schedule 1 are included.

Inspection records

Some equipment needs inspections between thorough examinations. The HSE says the need for and frequency of these should be decided through risk assessment. If you carry out inspections for customers, store those records alongside the examination reports.

Using due dates to help customers and win repeat work

The HSE sets out the intervals for regular thorough examinations, unless an examination scheme specifies otherwise:

Equipment Interval
Lifting equipment and accessories used to lift people Every 6 months
All lifting accessories Every 6 months
All other lifting equipment Every 12 months

Examinations are also needed in other situations, including after assembly at each new location for equipment that needs it, and after exceptional circumstances such as damage, long periods out of use or major changes.

Because every report includes the date the next examination is due, your portal can do something useful with it:

  • show each item's next due date and a clear status (current, due soon, overdue)
  • send reminder emails to the customer's nominated contact before the due date
  • let the customer book the next examination with one click

This turns the portal from a filing cabinet into a service tool, and it keeps your inspection diary full. The customer remains responsible for their own legal duties, so word reminders as a helpful service, not a guarantee.

How the portal should work

Organised by equipment, not just by order

Customers think in terms of "the 2 tonne chain block in bay 3", not order numbers. Give each item a record with its serial or ID number, description, safe working load, location if the customer wants to record it, and every document linked to it over its life: the DoC, examination reports, inspection records and any repair notes.

Easy search and export

Search by serial number, equipment type, site or due date. Let customers download a single certificate as a PDF or export a register of all their equipment for an audit.

Multiple users per customer

Larger customers have several sites and several people who need access. Let a customer administrator add and remove their own users, and limit some users to particular sites.

Records that can't be changed

The HSE says records should be protected from unauthorised alteration. Make uploaded certificates read-only for customers, keep a history of every upload and replacement on your side, and never overwrite an old report when a new one arrives; add the new one alongside it.

Security and personal data

A portal holds business records and users' contact details, so treat it seriously: strong passwords, two-factor login for administrators, encrypted connections, regular backups and access logs. Collect only the user details you need, and link to your privacy notice from the login page.

Building it on WordPress and WooCommerce

You don't need custom enterprise software to start. On a WordPress or WooCommerce site, a portal can be built with:

  • customer accounts, extended from WooCommerce's existing "My Account" area, so trade customers use one login for ordering and certificates
  • an equipment record type with fields for serial number, description, SWL, location and next due date
  • secure document storage that only the right customer's users can access, with files served through the site rather than from public URLs
  • automatic linking so that when you sell an item, its DoC is attached to the customer's account straight away
  • scheduled reminder emails driven by each item's next due date

If you use inspection software already, the portal can often pull reports from it, so your engineers don't upload everything twice. My guide to automating workflows with Make and Zapier shows the kind of connections involved.

Frequently asked questions

Can lifting equipment certificates be kept electronically?

Yes. HSE guidance says examination and inspection records don't need to be kept in hard copy, but you should be able to provide a written copy when necessary, such as when the enforcing authority asks or equipment is hired out or sold. The records should also be protected from unauthorised alteration.

How often does lifting equipment need a thorough examination?

Unless an examination scheme sets other intervals, HSE says every 6 months for equipment and accessories used to lift people, every 6 months for all lifting accessories, and every 12 months for other lifting equipment. Examinations are also needed before first use in some cases, after assembly at a new location and after exceptional circumstances.

What must a LOLER thorough examination report include?

HSE explains the report must contain the information in LOLER Schedule 1, including the examination date, the date the next examination is due and any defects that are or could become a danger to people. There's no set format, provided all 11 items listed in the Schedule are included.

What should a lifting equipment certificate portal include?

A record for each item with its serial number and safe working load, linked Declarations of Conformity, examination reports and inspection records, next due dates with status and reminders, search and export tools, multiple users per customer with site-level access, read-only documents and secure, private file storage.
